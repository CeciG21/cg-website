Delivery Lens

An evidence-led approach to Agile delivery analysis. Historical snapshots, scope movement and optional AI-assisted interpretation support better-informed conversations about delivery and release readiness.

Next.js & TypeScriptAzure DevOpsPostgreSQLOptional AI
OVERVIEW

Delivery reporting grounded in historical evidence.

Delivery Lens examines how a sprint changes over time rather than relying solely on the current board. Its analysis distinguishes planned work, later additions, removals and the evidence available for release decisions.

ENGINEERING CHALLENGE

Today’s board cannot explain yesterday’s plan.

Current work-item states can obscure original commitments and subsequent scope changes. Reporting needs point-in-time data and an explicit distinction between confirmed and missing evidence.

WORKFLOW

How it works

STEP 01

Rebuild the baseline

Use historical Azure DevOps evidence to establish which work items were in scope at the selected cutoff.

STEP 02

Explain what changed

Track additions, removals and delivery signals separately instead of relying on a single headline total.

STEP 03

Interpret the evidence

Present clear indicators and allow optional AI explanations based on the same verified snapshot, without filling gaps with guesses.

ENGINEERING

Technical approach

Web application

Next.js and TypeScript for the interface and evidence-focused reporting workflow.

Data sources

Read-only Azure DevOps integration, with PostgreSQL for snapshot and analysis data.

Historical analysis

Point-in-time queries distinguish original sprint scope from later work-item movement.

AI boundaries

Optional AI interpretation is constrained by the available evidence; unknowns remain explicit.

WHY IT MATTERS

More reliable evidence supports better delivery decisions.

The work connects software architecture, delivery governance and applied AI. It focuses on traceable reasoning, clear uncertainty and information that teams can use to review progress without mistaking a narrative for a verified fact.
